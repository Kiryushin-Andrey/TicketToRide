The Garden on Maple Street

The community garden on Maple Street had been an empty lot for years — just weeds, broken glass, and a rusted shopping cart nobody claimed. Then one spring, Mrs. Alvarez planted a single row of sunflowers along the fence, just because she missed her grandmother's yard back home.

Neighbors stopped to ask what she was doing. Some laughed kindly. But by summer, the boy next door had added tomatoes. The retired teacher across the street brought in marigolds "to keep the bugs away." A family who'd just moved from three states over showed up one Saturday with a wheelbarrow full of soil and no plan at all, and somehow that turned into a plan.

By the time autumn came, the lot wasn't empty anymore. It was full — of vegetables, of laughter, of kids chasing each other between the rows, of strangers who now knew each other's names and borrowed each other's tools without asking twice.

Mrs. Alvarez still tends her sunflowers every morning. She says she only ever meant to grow a few flowers. She never expected to grow a whole neighborhood.
