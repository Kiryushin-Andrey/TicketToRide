# The Last Destination Card

Our board game night had run for eleven years. Nobody ever remembered who owned the old *Ticket to Ride* box we played from that night. It was just there on the shelf, its corners soft as cloth.

We were four players: Mara, Devin, Priya and me. We dealt the cards and picked our colors. Mara took red, as always.

"Five of us tonight," Devin said, counting the little plastic trains in the box. He dealt out five hands of destination cards.

"There are four of us," Priya said.

Devin frowned at the fifth stack on the table, then swept it back into the box without another word. We started to play.

---

By the third round the lights had dimmed. The bulb hadn't flickered. The room just seemed to be getting smaller around the table.

When I drew my destination cards, one of them wasn't from the deck. It was blank on the front, and on the back, in handwriting that looked a lot like mine, it read: **Here → Nowhere. 20 points.**

"Funny," I said, and I slid it under the others.

On the board, a line of black trains was growing across the map. None of us owned black. Every time I looked away and back, it had crept a city or two closer. It was heading toward the room where we were sitting, which the map somehow showed, in tiny print, between Winnipeg and Duluth.

"Who's playing black?" Mara whispered.

Nobody answered. Mara's red trains were quietly coming off the board, one at a time, though nobody had touched them.

---

Devin stood up to leave and sat back down. He did that twice. "The door's not where it was," he said, in a flat voice. We all turned and looked at the wall.

Priya reached for her cards and found six new ones in her hand, all blank. She turned over one, then another. Each said the same thing in the same handwriting: **Here → Nowhere.**

"It's finishing the route," I said. I don't know how I knew.

The black line was one station away now. The last tiles clicked into place with a sound like knuckles cracking, and the box on the shelf rattled its lid.

---

We never did learn who won.

The next morning the game was packed away in its box, and the box sat on the shelf with its corners soft as cloth. There were four of us, I think, because the count seemed right. Four chairs, four mugs, four colors.

But when I opened the box last week to play again, there were five trains missing from the tray. One of them was black, and it was sitting just outside the door.

*Your turn.*
