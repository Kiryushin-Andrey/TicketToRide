# The Great Caboose Incident

Somewhere between Vienna and Berlin, a junior conductor named Gerald discovered
that the "Destination Ticket" deck in Car 7 had been secretly replaced,
overnight, by a stack of parking tickets someone's cousin had been collecting
since 2019.

Nobody noticed for three full rounds.

Players dutifully drew their tickets, squinted at them, and began plotting
ambitious routes between "Lot C, Level 2" and "That One Spot Near the Mall
Where You Always Get Towed." One particularly competitive player — a man who
had, up to this point, been winning decisively — spent eleven minutes building
a flawless chain of blue and red train cars connecting Frankfurt to Munich,
only to flip his ticket and discover his actual objective was "Return to
Meter Before It Expires: 45 points."

He did not take this well. He accused the dealer of fraud. The dealer, who
was also Gerald, pointed out that he too held a ticket demanding he connect
"Driveway" to "Still Driveway, But The Other End," worth a humiliating 2
points, and that he had not complained nearly as loudly.

The game was eventually won by a woman named Priscilla who had been
stacking train cars in a neat little pyramid the entire time, not realizing
this was not a valid strategy, and who was declared the winner by default
when everyone else got into an argument about whether a parking garage
counts as a "major city" for scoring purposes.

The moral of the story, as recorded in the official rulebook amendment that
nobody asked for: always check your deck before shuffling, and never, under
any circumstances, let Gerald near the cards again.

**THE END** 🚂🎫
