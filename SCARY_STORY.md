# The Last Train to Nowhere

Our board game group had a rule: nobody leaves until the last route is claimed.

That night, the rule turned out to be a mistake.

It was a Ticket to Ride night in November. Rain hammered the windows of Marta's old farmhouse, the power flickered twice, and then the lights went out for good. We lit candles and kept playing, because the game was close and nobody wanted to be the first to quit.

"Your turn, Dan," Marta said.

Dan drew a ticket card and went pale. "This isn't one of ours."

He turned it around. It was a destination ticket, printed on yellowed card in a faded hand. **Hollow Creek to Ashgrave: 40 points.** There was no Hollow Creek on our map, and no Ashgrave either.

"Funny," said Priya. "Which expansion is that?"

Nobody knew. We counted the deck and it had one card too many.

---

We shrugged it off and kept playing, which was the second mistake.

The next time I looked at the board, there was a track on it I had never seen. It was a thin black line that ran off the edge of the map and across the table, and it ended at the dark window. I was sure nobody had placed it.

"Who put a train there?" I asked.

"Put a train where?" said Marta.

Nobody else could see it. When I blinked, it was gone.

Then the candles all leaned toward the window at once, as if something outside had taken a breath.

---

Dan was the first to go.

He said he needed air. He stood up, took the strange ticket with him, and walked to the front door. We heard it open, and we heard rain. We didn't hear the door close.

We found his chair empty and his plastic trains lined up in a perfect row. They were all black and all pointing at the door.

"That's not funny, Dan!" Priya shouted into the dark porch. Nobody answered.

Far off, past the fields where there was no railway and never had been, a train whistle sounded.

---

Priya went next. She followed the whistle and said she'd bring him back. Marta and I stayed at the table and held hands across the board, and we tried very hard not to look at the line of black track that had come back and was creeping closer to our corner of the map.

"Marta," I whispered, "how many cards are in the deck now?"

She counted with shaking fingers.

"Four players," she said. "Four tickets. And one more."

She turned the last card over.

It showed my name in the same faded handwriting. **Home to Ashgrave: 40 points.** Underneath, in smaller letters, it said: *Route incomplete. One passenger remaining.*

The whistle was right outside now. The black track ran up the table leg, across the board and into my hand, cold as iron. Through the window I could see a long, pale train with no engine. Every carriage was lit, and in every window sat a player from some other game night, waiting.

Dan waved from the third car. Priya sat beside him. The seat next to them was empty.

---

Marta says she remembers the rest differently. She says the power came back at dawn and I was asleep at the table with my head on the board. She says Dan and Priya went home early, and she doesn't know why I keep asking about them.

But the game is still set up on her table, and I have checked it many times. Dan's black trains are still lined up in their row. The track still runs off the edge of the map, and one more route has been claimed.

It's the one that leads to my house.

**The End.**
