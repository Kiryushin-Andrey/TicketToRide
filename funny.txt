The Great Ticket to Ride Incident

Four friends sat down for a "quick" game of Ticket to Ride that was supposed to last thirty minutes before dinner.

Dave drew a route card for San Francisco to New York and immediately declared himself "basically a railroad tycoon now." He spent the next twenty minutes hoarding every locomotive card like a dragon guarding gold, refusing to build a single track "for strategic reasons."

Meanwhile, Priya quietly built a route directly through the exact cities Dave needed, humming innocently the whole time. When Dave finally noticed his path was blocked, he stood up so fast his chair fell over, pointed at the board, and shouted "THIS IS SABOTAGE" as if she'd committed a war crime instead of playing a board game correctly.

Marcus, who had been "just here for the snacks," accidentally completed two long routes without realizing it and won by a landslide. He didn't even know what a locomotive card did. When asked his secret strategy, he said, "I just put trains where there were holes."

Dave demanded a rematch. That was four hours ago. Dinner is cold. Priya has since built a second empire. Dave has flipped his chair over one more time. The dog left the room out of secondhand embarrassment.

The moral of the story: never trust a man who calls dibs on train routes before the rules have even been explained.
