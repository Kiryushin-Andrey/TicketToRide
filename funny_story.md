# The Great Train Disaster of Gary's Living Room

Gary had been planning his masterpiece for weeks: a single, unbroken railway from Lisbon to Moscow, 45 trains long, with zero detours.

"Nobody can stop me," he told his cat, Duchess. Duchess blinked slowly, which Gary took as a sign of respect. It was actually a sign of boredom.

On turn 12, Gary's sister Maria calmly claimed the one track he needed, the Kyiv–Smolensk route. She did it while eating a sandwich, which Gary considered an act of war.

"You *monster*," he whispered.

"It's three points, Gary."

"It's *my* three points!"

Gary spent the next six turns drawing cards in grim silence. Then Duchess jumped onto the table, skidded across the board, and scattered every little plastic train into the void beneath the couch.

Everyone froze.

Duchess sat down in the middle of Europe and began to clean her paw. She was now, by any reasonable reading of the rules, standing on every city at once.

"Well," said Maria, "she just completed the longest route."

"She doesn't even have *trains*!"

"She has *vibes*, Gary."

The judges (the group chat) ruled in favor of Duchess, 4–1. Gary's lone vote for himself was struck down as "biased."

Gary now plays only solitaire. Duchess still wins.

**THE END**
