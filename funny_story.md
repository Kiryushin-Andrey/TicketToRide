# The Great Ticket to Ride Incident

Dave had been playing board games for twenty years, but nothing prepared him for the night his cat, Sir Reginald Fluffington III, decided to audit his train routes.

It started innocently enough. Dave laid out his claimed route from Seattle to New York — a masterpiece of seven interlocking train cards, three transfers, and one deeply satisfying tunnel through Denver. He leaned back, admired his work, and went to grab a snack.

Thirty seconds later, he returned to find Sir Reginald sitting directly in the middle of the board, grooming himself with the serene confidence of a cat who owns the entire Pacific Northwest.

"Reginald. Move."

Reginald blinked slowly, which in cat law translates to "I was here first, the lease is mine now."

Dave tried to nudge him. Reginald responded by batting a locomotive card off the table and underneath the couch, a location from which, as any homeowner knows, items do not return — they simply transcend to a different plane of existence.

"That was my wildcard!" Dave yelled.

His roommate Priya wandered in, took one look at the scene — a grown man negotiating with a cat over territorial rights to a cardboard map of America — and said, "Who's winning?"

"The cat," Dave admitted. "The cat is always winning."

Priya sat down to play the human opponent's turn, since apparently someone still needed to. She drew her cards, glanced at the board, and declared she was building a route from Los Angeles to Chicago.

Reginald immediately relocated to sit on Chicago.

"He's blocking you now," Dave said. "I think he's sponsored by Seattle."

"He's not sponsored by anything, he's a cat."

"He has motive. He has opportunity. He has a monopoly on the Rocky Mountains."

They ultimately resolved the dispute the way all great board game disputes are resolved: by building the remainder of the route around the cat, treating him as a sort of fixed, immovable geographical feature — official games henceforth referred to him as "Lake Reginald."

Dave lost the game. He blamed Lake Reginald. Lake Reginald, for his part, offered no comment, having fallen asleep directly on top of the destination tickets, which by house rule now also belonged to him.

They never did find the locomotive card. Priya swears she still hears it rustling under the couch sometimes, calling out for backup.
