# The Great Train Delay of Pigeon Junction

Barnaby Plunkett had one goal: connect Seattle to New York with a single, glorious, uninterrupted railway line, and win Ticket to Ride before his sister Dorothea finished her third cup of tea.

"You're blocking me," said Dorothea, calmly placing six yellow trains between Chicago and Pittsburgh.

"I am not blocking you. I am *strategically occupying* the route."

"You've been strategically occupying it for forty minutes. You're holding a card."

Barnaby looked down. He was indeed holding a card. It was a locomotive, and he had been using it as a bookmark, a coaster, and, briefly, a tiny hat for the cat.

The cat, Sir Reginald Whiskers III, chose that moment to walk across the board. Forty-two plastic trains scattered like startled sheep. Montreal ended up in Miami. A single orange train lodged itself in Barnaby's tea.

"Well," said Dorothea, "that's the end of your Seattle–New York ticket."

"It is NOT. I'm simply... *re-routing through Florida*."

"There is no route through Florida to Seattle."

"Not with that attitude, there isn't."

They argued for another hour. In the end, Sir Reginald settled it by falling asleep on the Longest Route bonus card, which neither sibling dared to move.

Final score: Cat 0, Dorothea 112, Barnaby 47 (and a soggy orange train).

**The moral:** Never trust a man who uses a locomotive card as a hat.

*The End.*
