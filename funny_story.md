# The Rubber Duck Incident

A junior developer named Mark had heard about "rubber duck debugging" — the practice of explaining your code line by line to an inanimate object (traditionally a rubber duck) until you spot the bug yourself.

Mark didn't have a rubber duck, so he grabbed the nearest thing on his desk: a small cactus in a pot.

For three days, Mark explained his code to the cactus every time he got stuck. He narrated loops, justified variable names, and once apologized out loud to it for a particularly ugly nested conditional. His coworkers assumed he was on a call with headphones in.

On day four, his manager walked by during one of these sessions and overheard Mark saying, very seriously, "Okay, I know this looks like a race condition, but trust me, it's fine."

The manager peeked at the monitor. There was, in fact, a race condition.

The cactus, to its credit, said nothing — which Mark later admitted was the most useful code review feedback he'd gotten all week.

The team bought him an actual rubber duck the next day, with a sticky note on it that said: "For emergencies. The cactus files formal complaints now."
