THE GREAT OFFICE CHAIR INCIDENT

Gary from Accounting had one job that Tuesday: roll his chair six feet to the printer, grab his expense report, and roll back. He had done this a thousand times. He would not do it a thousand and first time without incident.

The trouble began when Gary, mid-roll, decided to check his phone instead of looking where he was going. His chair — a top-of-the-line "ErgoMax 3000" that his office manager Denise had bought specifically because it had "superior lumbar support and a frictionless gliding base" — lived up to its marketing in the worst possible way. It glided. Frictionlessly. Directly into the break room, through the open door, and straight toward the snack table, where it collided with a folding table holding, among other things, a sheet cake that said "CONGRATULATIONS ON 10 YEARS, BARBARA."

Barbara, who was standing two feet away mid-sentence about her decade of loyal service, watched the cake rotate once in the air like a frosted dinner plate in a magic trick, and land — intact, upright, miraculously undisturbed — directly in Gary's lap.

There was a full three seconds of silence.

Then Kevin from IT, who had been filming the whole thing on his phone for reasons nobody will ever fully understand, said, "Guys. Guys. I got it. I got all of it."

Gary sat there, frosting slowly sliding down his shirt, chair still gently rolling backward from momentum, phone still in his hand, the unread text message "did you grab my report??" still glowing on the screen.

"Happy anniversary, Barbara," he said, with the quiet dignity of a man who had accepted his fate.

Barbara, to her enormous credit, grabbed a fork from the snack table, walked over, and took a bite directly off Gary's shirt.

"Ten out of ten," she said. "Best cake delivery this company's ever done."

HR sent an email the following Monday. Subject line: "Reminder: Please Do Not Operate Rolling Chairs While Distracted." It was, by unanimous office vote, completely ignored, framed, and hung in the break room next to a photo of Gary, cake-covered, grinning like a man who had just discovered his true calling.

The ErgoMax 3000 was quietly retired to the supply closet. Denise insists this was unrelated to the incident. Nobody believes her. The chair, for its part, remains frictionless to this day — a monument to glorious, unearned confidence, waiting for its next victim.
