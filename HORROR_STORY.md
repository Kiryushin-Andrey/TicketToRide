# The Last Train to Nowhere

The board game had been in the attic for decades. Nobody remembered buying it.

Mara found it during a power cut, the box soft with damp, the lid stamped with a route map she didn't recognize. The cities had no names. Only small, careful drawings of windows.

"Four players," said her brother Tom, counting the pieces by candlelight. "Weird. There are only three of us."

They played anyway. Tom took the red trains, Mara the blue, and their cousin Jess the green. The fourth set of pieces, black and cold to the touch, stayed in the box.

On the second turn, Mara noticed a black train sitting on the board. It was on the long route between two of the window-cities, and it had been placed with great care.

"Which of you did that?" she asked.

Nobody had.

They laughed it off. Then Jess drew a destination ticket and went quiet. She turned it over and slid it across the table. In neat handwriting it said:

*Connect the attic to the people inside it.*

The candle guttered. Outside, the wind stopped, and the silence was so complete that they could hear the house breathing.

"Okay, that's enough," said Tom, and reached for the box to pack it up.

The black trains were already moving. They crossed the board in the dark between one blink and the next, closing every route, until a single line of black ran from the attic window to three small red, blue and green pieces huddled together in the middle of the map.

Mara realized the pieces weren't playing tokens. They were *them*.

Something heavy took a step on the stairs. Then another.

The ticket in Jess's hand rewrote itself while they watched: *Route complete. Final score: 3.*

In the morning the power came back on. The attic was empty, and the box sat closed on the table with its lid stamped with a new map. This time one of the windows had a name.

It was their street.

And the box held four sets of pieces again, black, red, blue and green, with room for one more.
