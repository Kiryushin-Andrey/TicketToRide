# The Great Ticket to Ride Incident of Game Night

It started, as these things do, with someone insisting they "basically invented" Ticket to Ride strategy after playing it exactly once. Game night had been going fine up until that point — chips were out, the board was set up, and nobody had yet said anything that would need to be apologized for later. That streak was about to end.

Dave had read a blog post. One blog post. Written, as far as anyone could tell, by a guy whose username was `TrainGoBrrr`. Armed with this singular piece of internet wisdom, Dave announced before the first card was even dealt that he would be "hoarding wild cards" and "controlling the board psychologically." Nobody knew what that meant. Dave didn't either.

Twenty minutes in, Dave had seven rainbow locomotive cards, zero train routes claimed, and a facial expression best described as "man watching his own funeral from a slight distance." Meanwhile Priya, who had said nothing about strategy and simply liked trains, quietly annexed the entire western half of the map like a tiny cardboard Napoleon. She did this while eating chips. With one hand.

"I'm playing the long game," Dave said, as Priya claimed Seattle to Los Angeles directly in front of his unclaimed route, forcing him into a 32-point detour through what the board insisted was a train line but what everyone agreed was functionally just "a suggestion."

Things escalated when John, three glasses of wine deep, began assigning dramatic backstories to his train routes. "This isn't just Denver to Helena," he announced, slamming a card down like a man closing an interrogation. "This is REVENGE." Nobody asked revenge for what. John did not offer further information. He simply stared at the little plastic train as if it owed him money.

By the end of the game, Dave had used his seven locomotives to build a single, glorious, utterly pointless route connecting two cities nobody needed, which he described — unprompted, misty-eyed — as "a monument to pure potential." Priya won by forty points without once raising her voice or putting down her chips. John's "revenge" route scored him eight points and, he claimed, "emotional closure."

The destination tickets were tallied. Scores were read aloud. Dave demanded a rematch "once everyone had studied more."

Nobody had studied. Nobody was going to study. The trains were put back in the box, mostly intact, except for one small blue train that Priya's cat had, at some point during turn six, silently stolen and was later found guarding it under the couch like contraband.

They played again the following week. Dave hoarded locomotives again. It did not go better. But he said the blog post "had more nuance than people gave it credit for," and really, isn't that the real journey we all took?
