# The Great Pittsburgh Incident

Four developers sat down for a "quick" game of Ticket to Ride during lunch break. It was supposed to take twenty minutes.

Dave drew a destination ticket: New York to Los Angeles. He groaned so loudly that someone two cubicles over asked if the build had failed again.

Maria, playing it cool, had quietly been hoarding black train cars for six turns "for a surprise later." The surprise, it turned out, was that she'd forgotten which route she was saving them for, and spent the next three turns squinting at the board muttering "it was definitely... one of the long ones."

Then came The Pittsburgh Incident. Sam, reaching across the table for the card deck, knocked over his coffee directly onto the Pittsburgh–Chicago route marker. In the ensuing panic to blot it up with napkins, someone's elbow swept half the discard pile onto the floor, where it slid directly under the snack table — a location universally agreed to be a Bermuda Triangle from which no card had ever returned.

Forty-five minutes later, lunch break long over, the four of them were on their hands and knees under the snack table, a facilities manager had wandered by twice looking concerned, and Dave — who still had not connected New York to Los Angeles — was overheard saying, "I just need ONE more turn," which is the exact sentence every board game disaster in human history has been preceded by.

They never did find the Chicago–Toronto card. Legend says it's still under there, slowly gathering crumbs, waiting for someone brave enough to move the snack table.

The game was declared a draw. Nobody was happy about it. They scheduled a rematch for the following Tuesday.
