# The Great Train Route Dispute of Bumblewick

Gerald had one goal in life: connect Bumblewick to Lower Snoring with a single, uninterrupted railway line of exactly 45 plastic trains.

He had 44.

"It's fine," he told his wife Marjorie, staring at the board. "I'll just... borrow one."

"From whom?"

Gerald looked across the table at his nephew Timmy, age nine, who was guarding a tidy pile of yellow trains like a dragon on a hoard of gold.

"Absolutely not," said Timmy, who had already heard the question in Gerald's eyes.

"I'll give you my Seattle-to-New-York ticket. It's worth 22 points."

"It's worth *negative* 22 points, Uncle Gerald, because you haven't finished it. You've been building the same blue route since lunch."

This was true. It was now 9 p.m.

Marjorie, who had been quietly winning all evening by claiming every short, boring, two-train route on the map, sipped her tea. "You know, dear, you could just take the other path through Duluth."

"I REFUSE to go through Duluth," said Gerald. "Duluth is where dreams go to idle."

"Duluth has three open routes," said Timmy.

"Then Duluth can keep them."

At that moment the family cat, Sir Reginald Fluffington III, leapt onto the table, slid across Nebraska, and scattered all of Gerald's trains across the carpet. He paused, looked Gerald dead in the eye, and knocked the 44th train off the edge of the board with one calm paw.

"He did that on purpose," whispered Gerald.

"He's *blocking* you," said Timmy, impressed. "That's actually a really good move."

Marjorie laid down her final cards, announced the game over, and claimed the Longest Route bonus with a route that, as far as anyone could tell, simply went in a small circle around Kansas.

"That's not even a real route," Gerald protested.

"It's 47 trains long and totally legal," said Marjorie, brushing a cat hair off her scorecard. "Read the rules, Gerald. Page 4."

Gerald read page 4. Then page 5, just in case. Then he put the rulebook down, picked up the lone remaining yellow train Timmy had finally, mercifully tossed him out of pity, and placed it triumphantly in Bumblewick.

"Well," he said, "it's not connected to anything. But it's *mine*."

Sir Reginald Fluffington III knocked it off the table.

**THE END**

*(Gerald lost by 112 points. He demanded a rematch. The cat was not invited.)*
