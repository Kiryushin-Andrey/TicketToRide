# The Great Train Delay of Gerald

Gerald had one goal in life: to connect Seattle to Miami using exactly 45 plastic trains and zero emotional damage.

He had planned the route for weeks. Seattle → Helena → Denver → Oklahoma City → Atlanta → Miami. It was beautiful. It was efficient. It was a masterpiece of cardboard logistics.

Then Brenda, his sister-in-law, placed a single blue train on Denver → Oklahoma City.

"Oh, was that your route?" she said, not looking up from her snack. "Whoops."

Brenda had never once said "whoops" and meant it.

Gerald took a deep breath. He had a backup plan: go through Kansas City. He reached for the pile of red cards he had been hoarding like a dragon hoards gold, only to discover that his nephew Timmy, age 7, had been using them as a "fort wall" for his gummy bears.

"They're guarding the candy," Timmy explained, with the gravity of a tiny general.

The red cards were sticky. Gerald now held a hand that was 40% sugar.

By turn 12, Gerald's longest route was a sad, three-station line between two towns nobody had ever heard of, which he had built out of pure desperation. His destination ticket read *Seattle to Miami (22 points)*. His actual achievement read *Duluth to Winnipeg, (a lovely place, I'm told)*.

When the final scores were tallied, Brenda won with 183 points. Timmy came second, mostly because he'd claimed every route in the game by sitting on the board.

Gerald came last, but he did earn one small victory: the "Longest Continuous Path" bonus, which he'd won entirely by accident when he linked his three sad towns to the sticky candy-fort stations.

"Ten points," he whispered. "TEN POINTS."

"Congratulations," said Brenda. "Rematch?"

Gerald looked at the board. He looked at Brenda. He looked at the gummy bear that was, somehow, standing on Chicago.

"Same time next week," he said.

*The end. Gerald is still waiting for his train to arrive.*
