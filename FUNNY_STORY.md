# The Great Railway Dispute of Tuesday

Gerald had been planning his route from Lisbon to Moscow for three hours. It was a masterpiece: a long, elegant chain of tracks winding through twelve cities, worth 41 points.

"Your turn," said Brenda, not looking up from her snack.

Gerald placed his final train cars with the solemnity of a man signing a peace treaty. "Behold," he said. "The Longest Route."

Brenda blinked. "Gerald, that's the route I finished an hour ago."

"No, that one's yours. This one is mine."

"They're the same track."

"They are *parallel* tracks."

"There is one track."

Gerald looked down. Brenda was right. Somewhere around hour two, he had been lovingly building on top of her trains, which he had mistaken for his own because, in his words, "they were also red."

"I'm green," said Brenda.

"I'm colorblind," said Gerald.

"You've been colorblind since the game started?"

"Only when I'm losing."

Silence fell. Their friend Dmitri, who had been quietly hoarding every locomotive card on the table, finally spoke. "I would like to point out that I'm winning."

"You're holding eleven wild cards and haven't built anything," said Brenda.

"It's called *strategy*."

"It's called *hoarding*."

"It's called *capitalism*," said Dmitri, and ate a card by accident.

The game was never officially finished. The final score was recorded as Gerald: 41 (disputed), Brenda: 41 (also disputed), and Dmitri: -1 (for eating a card). The group agreed on a rematch, and that next time they would play with the lights on.

Gerald still insists he would have won.

*The End.*
