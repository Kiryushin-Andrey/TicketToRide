# The Great Ticket to Ride Incident

Four friends sat down to play Ticket to Ride on a Friday night. Dave, a backend engineer, had spent all week debugging a routing algorithm at work. When it was his turn, he stared at the board for a solid two minutes, then announced, "I'm going to claim the shortest path between Chicago and New York."

"It's a board game, Dave," said Priya. "Just place your trains."

"I AM placing my trains," Dave said. "Optimally."

Meanwhile, Marcus had been secretly hoarding locomotive cards for eleven turns, convinced he was building up to the legendary San Francisco–New York route. When he finally laid them all down with a triumphant flourish, Priya calmly pointed out that someone had claimed that exact route three turns ago while he was "optimizing."

Marcus stared at his hand of now-useless cards, then at the board, then back at his hand.

"I've made a terrible mistake," he said, and slowly began eating a locomotive card.

It was, in fact, a felt coaster shaped like a locomotive card, which he'd mistaken for the real thing after two glasses of wine. The table fell apart laughing, Dave forgot all about his optimal pathfinding, and the game ended with nobody quite remembering who actually won — though everyone agreed Marcus's coaster had the longest continuous route of the night.
