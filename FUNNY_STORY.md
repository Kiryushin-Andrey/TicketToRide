# The Great Ticket to Ride Incident of Route 7

Dmitri had been staring at his hand of train cars for eleven minutes when
Yelena finally said what everyone was thinking.

"It's locomotives. You have six locomotives. Just play a locomotive."

"I'm *optimizing*," Dmitri said, in the tone of a man who had never
optimized anything in his life.

Across the table, Pavel was quietly building a route from Vancouver to
Vancouver. Not to anywhere near Vancouver — to Vancouver itself, by way of
Seattle, Calgary, Helena, and, for reasons no one could explain, Duluth.

"That's not a route," Yelena said. "That's a cry for help shaped like a
train."

"It's a *scenic* route," Pavel said, laying down three yellow cars with
the quiet dignity of a man defending a PhD thesis.

Meanwhile, the Destination Tickets sat face-down in the middle of the
table like a pile of tiny betrayals. Everyone had drawn at least one card
that said, essentially, "connect two cities you will never, ever reach,"
and everyone had kept it anyway, because folding felt like admitting
defeat to a cardboard map of 1900s America.

Dmitri finally played his locomotive. On a route that didn't need a
locomotive. To block Yelena, who wasn't even going that way.

"Why," Yelena said, not as a question.

"I panicked," Dmitri admitted. "You looked like you were about to do
something clever and I couldn't allow that."

Pavel, three turns from completing his Vancouver-to-Vancouver loop of
shame, looked up and said with total sincerity:

"This is the happiest I have been all week."

Nobody argued. Someone dealt new tickets. The trains kept not going
anywhere useful, and everyone agreed that was, somehow, the whole point.
