# The Great Train Route Dispute

Gerald the conductor had one job: get the 7:42 express from Seattle to Miami.

He had a map, a ticket, and 45 little plastic trains. Unfortunately, he also had his cousin Dave.

"I'll just take the long way," Dave said, laying a line of trains across Montana. "It's scenic."

"Dave, that's a *blocked route*. You're building a railroad through my sandwich."

"Your sandwich was on the map. That's on you."

By Nevada, Gerald realized Dave had been quietly collecting only yellow cards for an hour. "Are you hoarding locomotives?"

"Define hoarding."

"You have eleven wild cards."

"I have *options*."

Meanwhile, passenger Brenda needed to reach Boston, and was now being routed through Winnipeg "for strategic reasons." She filed a complaint with the only authority present: a pigeon on the platform. The pigeon ruled in favor of nobody and stole a fry.

At last, Gerald completed his route to Miami with one train left. Dave completed his own — a 3-mile track from Denver to... Denver.

"That's worth zero points," said Gerald.

"It's worth *emotional* points."

The final score was Gerald 112, Brenda 98, Dave −4, and the pigeon, somehow, 15.

Nobody asked how.

*The End. Please return your trains to the box.*
