# The Great Train Heist of Cheddar Junction

Gerald the conductor had one rule: never be late. Unfortunately, Gerald's train was carrying 4,000 wheels of cheese, and Gerald had just discovered that the track to Cheddar Junction was blocked, not by a landslide, but by Brenda, who had parked her train there to "finish a sandwich."

"Brenda, move your train," said Gerald.

"I'm on a claimed route," said Brenda, chewing. "Read the rules. Eighteen points for the Seattle–Cheddar line. I've worked hard for this."

"It's a *sandwich*, Brenda."

"It's a *strategy*."

Meanwhile, Dennis, a passenger holding a destination ticket for a city that did not exist ("Lower Upper Fenwick"), had been wandering between carriages for three hours asking everyone how to get there. Nobody knew. A goat knew, but the goat wasn't talking.

Things escalated quickly:

1. Gerald tried to go around Brenda. There was no "around". There was only a map with one dotted line and a coffee stain.
2. Brenda drew a card. It was a locomotive. She screamed. Everyone screamed. Nobody knew why.
3. The cheese began to sweat in the sun, and the smell attracted a flock of very determined seagulls, hundreds of miles from any sea.

At sunset, Dennis finally cracked the case. "Lower Upper Fenwick," he announced, "is just the name of the *dining car*."

He had been standing in it the whole time.

Brenda, moved by the revelation, finally released the track. Gerald arrived at Cheddar Junction forty minutes late, to a cheering crowd, a parade, and a mayor who had never once checked a timetable.

"Were we late?" asked Gerald.

"Late for what?" said the mayor, already holding a cracker.

**The End.**

*Moral: always read the route card, and never trust a sandwich.*
