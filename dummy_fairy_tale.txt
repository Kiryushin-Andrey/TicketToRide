The Little Engine That Could

Once upon a time, in a small town nestled between green hills, there was a little blue engine named Pip. Pip was not the fastest engine, nor the strongest, but he had the biggest heart in the whole rail yard.

One winter morning, a long train full of toys and sweets for the children on the other side of the mountain broke down. The big, shiny engines were too busy or too proud to help pull it over the steep slope.

"I am too grand for such a little train," said the big engine.
"I must rest, I am tired," said another.

At last, someone asked the little blue engine, "Will you try?"

Pip looked up at the tall mountain ahead. It seemed impossible. But he thought of the children waiting for their toys, and he said, "I think I can. I think I can."

Slowly, Pip began to pull. The train was heavy, and the mountain was steep, but Pip kept chugging along, whispering to himself, "I think I can, I think I can, I think I can."

Up and up he climbed, past the frosty pines and over the rocky path. Just as his wheels grew tired and his boiler grew warm, he reached the very top of the mountain.

As he rolled down the other side toward the waiting village, Pip smiled and said, "I thought I could, I thought I could, I thought I could."

And from that day on, Pip was known throughout the land not as the fastest or the strongest, but as the little engine with the biggest heart — the one who never gave up.

THE END
