The Great Ticket to Ride Tournament of Destiny

It was a dark and stormy Tuesday — or maybe it was Wednesday, nobody could agree, because the office calendar app had been down since the Great Printer Incident of last March. Four coworkers, Dave, Priya, Logan, and a suspiciously competitive intern named Clem, gathered around a Ticket to Ride board during lunch break, each silently convinced they were a secret railroad tycoon trapped in the body of a mid-level analyst.

Dave, who had read exactly one strategy blog post the night before, announced with the confidence of a man who had read two, "I'm going for the long southern route. Nobody takes the long southern route."

"Nobody takes it," Priya said, sliding three black train cards across the table, "because it's bad, Dave."

Logan said nothing. Logan was hoarding cards like a dragon hoards gold, occasionally cackling softly to himself, which everyone had agreed weeks ago to simply stop acknowledging.

Then there was Clem. Clem had looked up "optimal Ticket to Ride opening strategy" on his phone under the table and was now executing it with the grim precision of a Swiss watchmaker. Nobody suspected Clem. Clem was twenty-two and wore a cardigan. Cardigans do not plot.

Round four: Dave triumphantly laid down his final train car to complete the long southern route, threw his hands up, and declared himself "basically the Cornelius Vanderbilt of this table." At that exact moment, the office fire alarm went off — not because of the game, but because someone in the break room had attempted to microwave an entire baked potato wrapped in foil. The potato did not survive. Neither, metaphorically, did Dave's victory lap, since everyone had to evacuate to the parking lot mid-game.

Standing outside in the mild drizzle, train cards still clutched in hand like refugees fleeing a tiny cardboard nation, Priya turned to Logan and said, "You've been sitting on nine train cards of the same color for twenty minutes. What are you building, a monorail to Narnia?"

Logan simply smiled and said, "You'll see."

They never went back inside to finish the game. The fire department declared the building "potato-safe" forty minutes later, by which point everyone had lost the will to count routes and instead argued about whether pineapple belonged on pizza, a debate with significantly higher stakes and zero rulebook.

To this day, Logan still won't say what he was building. Clem quietly deleted his search history. And Dave still tells people he "basically won," which is, strictly speaking, a geographic impossibility, since the game never actually ended.

Moral of the story: in Ticket to Ride, as in the office, the real longest route is the one between you and finishing anything without an emergency evacuation.
