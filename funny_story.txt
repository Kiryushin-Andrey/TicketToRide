The Great Office Printer Heist

It was a Tuesday, which everyone knows is the most dangerous day of the week in corporate America, when Dave from Accounting first noticed the printer on the third floor was missing.

Not broken. Not jammed. Missing. As in, there was a printer-shaped dust outline on the carpet and nothing else.

Dave, a man who had never once instigated drama in his eleven years at the company, decided this was the hill he would die on. He called an emergency meeting. Six people showed up, three of whom were only there for the free donuts he'd promised in the calendar invite.

"Someone," Dave announced, pacing in front of a whiteboard, "took Gerald."

Gerald was the printer's name. Nobody remembered naming it Gerald, but by unanimous unspoken agreement, it had always been Gerald, and Gerald was gone.

The investigation that followed involved:
- Security footage that showed nothing because the camera had been pointed at a potted plant since March
- An intern named Kyle who confessed to "borrowing" Gerald for a student film project, then immediately un-confessed when he realized Gerald weighed as much as a washing machine and he did not, in fact, have a student film project
- A very confusing twenty minutes where everyone searched the supply closet, found a SECOND printer nobody knew existed, and briefly forgot they were looking for the first one

Gerald was eventually found in the break room, unplugged, wearing a sombrero that someone had left over from Cinco de Mayo, with a Post-it note taped to the paper tray that read: "On vacation. Please print responsibly. - Gerald"

To this day, nobody has admitted to moving him. HR opened a case file. The case file is still open. Dave checks on Gerald every morning before he gets coffee, just to be sure.

The sombrero remains. Some say it always will.
