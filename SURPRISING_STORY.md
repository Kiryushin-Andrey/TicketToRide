# The Longest Route

Marta had played Ticket to Ride every Friday for eleven years, and she had never once lost to her neighbor Hollis. He was a retired railway dispatcher. Everyone agreed this made it worse.

Tonight she drew her destination tickets and went pale: Vancouver to Miami, Seattle to New York, and Los Angeles to Montreal. All three were long routes, and all three crossed the same bottleneck.

"Something wrong?" Hollis asked, with a faint smile.

"Nothing at all."

She began laying track in a hurry. By the fourth round she had spotted something odd. Hollis had stopped building. He kept drawing cards and hoarding them, and he looked at the board more than at his hand.

"You're not even trying," she said.

"I am," he said. "I'm just not building."

On the last turn Marta finished her third ticket with a single blue train, and she whooped. Hollis turned over his destination tickets one by one. There were three, and every one was a route she had already built. Vancouver to Miami. Seattle to New York. Los Angeles to Montreal.

"Those are *my* tickets," she said.

"They were," Hollis said. "I swapped the decks while you were pouring the tea. I've spent eleven years watching you play, and I know exactly which routes you can't resist. I needed you to build mine for me, and you did. We've both completed all three, so tonight we tie."

Marta stared at him, then at the board, and then she laughed until she had to put her head on the table.

"You *cheated* your way into a draw?"

"I cheated my way into a draw," Hollis said, very pleased with himself. "Eleven years, Marta. It's the first time I haven't lost."

She poured him more tea. Then she reached into the box, took out the spare deck, and started shuffling.

"Rematch," she said. "And this time I'm watching the tea."
