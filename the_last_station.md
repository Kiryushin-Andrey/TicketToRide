# The Last Station

Marta kept the ticket in her coat pocket for forty years. It was for a train that never left — the 6:15 to Dovermere, cancelled the night the bridge went down, the same night her brother was supposed to come home from the war.

She never told anyone why she still rode the rails every evening at dusk, sitting on the same platform bench, watching the empty track. The other conductors knew her only as "the woman who waits," and after a while, they stopped asking.

Each October, when the leaves turned the color of rust, she would take the ticket out, trace the faded ink with her thumb, and whisper his name to the wind. "I kept your seat," she'd say. "I always kept your seat."

The station closed in the spring. They tore up the old platform to build a parking lot. Marta was there the morning the last beam came down, standing where the bench used to be, holding an empty space where forty years of waiting had worn a groove into her heart.

She folded the ticket one last time, set it gently on the gravel where the tracks had been, and walked home alone — carrying, for the first time, nothing at all.
