# The Last Train to Willow Creek

Every evening at dusk, old Tobias Marsh walked to the end of the platform at Willow Creek Station and waited for a train that was never going to come.

The line had closed eleven years earlier. The tracks were rusted orange, the timetable board had faded to a pale ghost of itself, and weeds pushed up through the cracks in the boards. But Tobias still wore his conductor's cap, still polished the brass buttons on his coat, and still checked his pocket watch against a clock that had stopped at 6:42.

His wife, Marguerite, had loved the 6:42. She would meet him at the platform every night, a thermos of tea in her hands, and they would ride together to the coast on Sundays, where she would press her face to the window like a child. "Again," she would say, every time the sea came into view. "Show me again."

When she grew ill, he took her on the train one last time. She was too weak to sit up, but he held her so she could see the water. "Again," she whispered. He promised he would.

The railway closed the spring after she died.

Now Tobias stood on the empty platform, lantern in hand, as the light drained from the sky. Some nights he thought he heard a whistle far away. Some nights he thought he saw a warm glow on the horizon.

It was always only the wind, and the fireflies in the tall grass.

One autumn evening, the neighbors found his cap resting on the bench at the end of the platform, the watch beside it, still ticking. The lantern was lit.

They say the station is quiet now. But if you stand there at 6:42, when the light is low, you might smell tea and hear a soft voice on the wind, saying:

*Again. Show me again.*
