# The Ticket That Never Rode

Old Mr. Hensley kept a dusty train schedule pinned above his desk, the corner curled from decades of thumbing past it. Every year on his late wife's birthday, he'd walk to the station and buy two tickets — one for himself, one for her, as if she might still be running late.

The clerks changed over the years, but they all learned to sell him the second ticket without a word, sliding it across the counter like it was the most ordinary thing in the world.

This year, when he reached into his coat for his wallet, his hands shook too much to count the coins. A young clerk he didn't recognize waited patiently, then quietly covered the difference herself.

Mr. Hensley rode the train alone, as he always did, the empty seat beside him holding nothing but the window light. When the conductor came to punch his ticket, he found the second one tucked into the seatback pocket, unused and folded with care, just like every year before.

He never told anyone who she was. He never needed to.
