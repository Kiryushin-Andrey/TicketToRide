# The Last Train to Nowhere

Every Sunday for forty years, Walter set up the Ticket to Ride board on the kitchen table. Two chairs, two sets of little plastic trains: his blue, Margaret's red.

Margaret always went for the long routes. "Anyone can connect two neighboring cities," she'd say, tapping her tickets with a smile. "I want to cross the whole country." She won more often than not, and she never once let him forget it.

Then one winter, the chair across from him stayed empty.

Walter kept setting up the board anyway. He dealt her cards and laid out her red trains in a neat row. Some Sundays he even took her turn, claiming the long route from Vancouver to Miami, the way she would have.

He never finished the route. One track short, every time. Some things are easier to leave unfinished.

On the last Sunday of the year, his granddaughter found him at the table. She said nothing. She pulled out the second chair, sat down, and picked up the red trains.

"Teach me," she said.

Walter's hands trembled as he handed her the cards. For the first time in months, he laid down a track that was not his own.

*The end.*
