# The Last Train to Millbrook

Old Walter had worked the Millbrook station for forty-one years. He knew every rail, every creaking board, every regular who passed through the turnstile with a nod.

Each evening at 6:15, the last train pulled in, and each evening Walter stood on the platform with a thermos of tea and two cups. One was for him. The other was for Margaret, his wife, who used to meet him there after her shift at the bakery so they could ride home together.

Margaret passed away in the spring. Walter kept bringing the second cup anyway.

When the railway company announced that Millbrook would close at the end of autumn, too few passengers and too much upkeep, nobody argued. The town had already thinned out, one shuttered shop at a time.

On the final night, the train arrived on schedule. The conductor, a young man Walter had once taught to read the signal lamps, stepped down and took off his cap.

"Last stop, Walter," he said softly.

Walter nodded, poured the tea into both cups, and set one on the bench beside him. The train waited longer than the timetable allowed. Then, with a long, low whistle, it pulled away into the dark.

The lamps went out one by one. The tea went cold.

Walter sat there until morning, and for the first time in forty-one years, nobody came to tell him it was time to go home.
