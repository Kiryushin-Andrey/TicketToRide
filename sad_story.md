# The Last Lighthouse Keeper

Old Tomas had tended the lighthouse at Gray Point for forty-one years. Every evening he climbed the 112 steps, lit the lamp, and watched the ships pass. He never knew their names, but he waved to each one anyway.

His wife, Elena, used to bring tea up to the lamp room. After she passed, he kept making two cups out of habit, and drank them both, one after the other, while the tea went cold.

One autumn, a letter arrived. The lighthouse was to be automated. A machine would do his job, and no one would need to climb the stairs again.

On his final night, Tomas polished the great lens until it shone. He lit the lamp, as he had done fourteen thousand times before, and sat with his two cups of tea.

Far out on the dark water, a single ship sounded its horn: one long note, then another. Tomas stood and waved, though nobody could see him.

In the morning, the keeper's cottage was empty. The lamp kept turning, steady and precise. Nobody was there to see it, and nobody waved back.
