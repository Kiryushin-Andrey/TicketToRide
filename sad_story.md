# The Last Train to Alder Creek

Alder Creek station had one train a day, and for forty years Mr. Hollis had waved at it from the platform.

He wasn't the conductor or the stationmaster. He was just a man who came every evening at six to watch the 6:05 pull in, because that was the train his wife had taken home from the city on the day they met.

She died in the spring. He kept coming anyway.

The railway announced that the line would close in autumn. Too few passengers, they said. On the final evening, the platform was empty except for Mr. Hollis, a thermos of tea, and two cups, one of which he set down on the bench beside him, as he always did.

The 6:05 arrived. No one got off. The conductor, who had seen Mr. Hollis every day for twelve years, leaned out and tipped his cap. Mr. Hollis raised his hand and waved.

The train pulled away, and its red light shrank down the track until it vanished into the trees.

Mr. Hollis sat for a long while. Then he poured the tea from the second cup back into the thermos, folded his scarf, and walked home in the dark.

**The End.**
