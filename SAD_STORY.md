# The Last Train to Nowhere

Every Friday for twenty years, Walter and Eleanor played Ticket to Ride at the kitchen table. Eleanor always chose the blue trains. Walter always chose the red ones and always lost.

"One day I'll complete Seattle to New York," he'd say, tracing the route with a finger.

"Not while I'm blocking Chicago," she'd answer, and slide a card across the table with a smile.

When Eleanor passed in the spring, the game stayed on the table. Walter couldn't bring himself to put it away. The blue trains sat in their little pile, waiting.

Months later, on a quiet Friday, he sat down, shuffled the cards, and dealt two hands. He played hers as well as his own, and he played it the way she would have. He blocked Chicago.

Walter finally completed Seattle to New York that night. No one was there to groan, and no one slid a card across the table.

He sat for a long while looking at the finished route. Then he gently placed a single blue train on the last empty spot beside it, so the line would run the whole way.

"Well played, El," he whispered.

The kitchen was very quiet. The trains stayed where they were.
