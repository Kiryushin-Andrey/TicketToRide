# The Last Train to Duluth

Marguerite had held the Seattle–Duluth route for eleven years of Friday game nights. Every Friday, the same four friends gathered around the same worn board, and every Friday she laid her blue trains one careful car at a time.

This year, there were three of them.

Walter's chair stayed empty. Nobody had the heart to move it, and nobody had the heart to sit in it. His yellow trains remained in the box, neatly stacked, the way he always kept them.

"Your turn," said Priya quietly, and no one pointed out that she had said it twice.

Walter had always been the one to block Marguerite's route at the last moment, cackling as he claimed the one track she needed. She had complained about it for a decade. Tonight she stared at the open stretch between Helena and Duluth, and nothing stood in her way.

She finished the route. It was the easiest she had ever made.

Nobody cheered. Priya counted the points, and Marguerite won by a wide margin.

Afterward, while the others put the cards away, Marguerite took a single yellow train from Walter's box. She set it on the empty track between Helena and Duluth, the one spot she had left open on purpose.

"Blocked," she whispered.

Then she turned off the light and went home.
