# The Last Train to Lower Snoring

Gerald set up the board on the kitchen table, the way he did every Sunday.

Four colors of trains. Four piles of cards. He laid them out out of habit, then stopped, his hand hovering over the green pieces, the ones Marjorie always chose.

For forty years she had taken the short, boring routes. Two trains here, two trains there. She never cared about the long, glorious lines Gerald dreamed up. "Someone has to hold the map together," she used to say, and she would win, quietly, with a small smile.

He put the green trains back in the box. Then he took them out again and set them in a neat pile at her empty chair.

The house was very quiet. Even Sir Reginald Fluffington III, who had once knocked an entire game onto the carpet out of spite, sat politely on the windowsill and did nothing.

Gerald drew his tickets. Bumblewick to Lower Snoring. 45 points, if he could make it.

He played slowly. He claimed the long blue route first, then the red one, then the gray. When he reached Duluth he stopped.

"I always said I'd never go through Duluth," he said to the room.

Nobody answered. But he could almost hear her: *Oh, Gerald. Just go through Duluth.*

So he did.

When the last train clicked into place, the line ran unbroken from one end of the board to the other. He sat looking at it for a long time. It was the best route he had ever built, and there was no one to show.

Timmy came by at dusk, taller than Gerald remembered, holding a box of yellow trains.

"I thought you might want a game," he said.

Gerald looked at the board, then at his nephew, then at the green pile sitting by the empty chair.

"You can have her trains," he said softly. "She'd want you to take the short routes. Somebody has to hold the map together."

Timmy sat down. Neither of them said anything for a while. Then Timmy picked up a green train, turned it over in his hand, and placed it carefully on the board.

Outside, the evening light faded. On the windowsill, the cat closed his eyes.

**THE END**
