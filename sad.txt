The Last Train

Every evening for forty years, Eleanor waited on the same platform bench, watching the 6:15 pull in. She wasn't going anywhere. She just liked to watch the doors open, hoping, in some small unspoken way, that her son would step off one of them.

He'd left on a train just like it, decades ago, after a fight neither of them ever got to take back. He'd meant to call. She'd meant to write. Years folded into each other the way they do when everyone assumes there's still time.

The letter came on a gray Tuesday. Not from him — about him. It had happened quietly, an ocean away, in a small apartment full of unopened boxes and one photograph of a woman on a platform bench.

Eleanor still goes to the station. She isn't waiting for him anymore. She just isn't sure how to stop.
